Transportation & Rail Leasing
The thesis. GATX is a good business at close to a fair price. It is the largest railcar lessor in North America, the fleet doubled in January through the Wells Fargo deal, utilization is 98% and the industry is not building enough cars. None of that is in dispute. What is in dispute is 2027 earnings. The Street has $11.27. My model, built from the 10-K and the Q2 10-Q, gets $10.18, and the gap is interest: the Wells fleet came with about $3B of GABX debt (now a $1.9B floating term loan plus $1B of fixed notes), and the interest on it eats most of what lease repricing adds. At GATX's own ten-year median multiple of 16.34×, my earnings are worth $166, and a vintage-marked net asset value agrees at $169. The buy-side lens is harsher: the stock trades at about 2.0× book, which needs a higher return on equity than the 12 to 13% the filings show. Justified P/B gives $158. Weighting the three (P/B 35%, NAV 35%, P/E 30%) gives $162 against $162.78. After the 4% drop on October 7 the price no longer pays for the Street's earnings, but it still pays for a return on equity the company does not earn. Close to fair, not cheap: above about $174 the same rule says SELL, below about $144 BUY. Fleet growth: GATX owns about a third of it (30% at close, 33.5% after buying more in June).

WHY THE PRICE IS CLOSE TO FAIR

  • Only the cars whose leases end this year get the new rate. Leases run about 54 months, so roughly a quarter of the fleet reprices in any year. The Lease Price Index (the change in rate on renewal) was +16.8% in Q2 2026, down from +24.5% a year earlier. On the renewing slice that is worth about +4% on the blended fleet rate, or roughly $31M of pretax to GATX in 2027, about $0.67 a share, and it fades as the cheap 2021 leases run out. The repricing story is real and it is already mostly in the numbers.
  • Interest is the line the Street is under-modeling. Debt went from $8.3B to $12.5B in one year. The 2026 maturities carry a 3.05% coupon and refinance near 5.30% to 5.95%. My model has 2027 interest at $616M, up about $224M from the pre-Wells run rate. Against about $31M of repricing, that is the whole $1.09 gap between my $10.18 and the Street's $11.27.
  • The earnings cushion is asset sales, not leasing. Disposition gains (selling old cars above book) are guided to $200M for 2026 and are about 40% of pretax income. They are recurring but cyclical. Core EPS with no gains is $5.57; at the six-year average of $105M of gains it is $7.81, which puts the stock at 22.0× normalized earnings.
  • The RRPF engine JV pays out less than it earns. GATX books 50% of the Rolls-Royce JV's profit as one line ($117.5M after tax in 2025) but received $50M of cash. The earnings are real; the cash conversion is 43%. I model dividends, not the accounting share, when I value it.
  • Brookfield owns two-thirds of the new fleet. GABX is 100% consolidated, so revenue, interest and utilization all look bigger, but 66.5% of its profit leaves through the non-controlling interest line. GATX also guarantees 100% of the GABX debt while owning about a third of the equity (30% at close, 33.5% after buying more in June). The call options to buy Brookfield out are real but undisclosed in price; the first tranche cost $66M for 3.5 points.
  • Four valuation methods: two now above the price, two below. P/E on my earnings $170. Net asset value with each vintage of the fleet marked to market $169. A seven-lessor P/B-versus-ROE regression $161 (weak fit, shown not weighted). Residual income, which asks what return on equity the price requires, $99: the price needs a return on equity well above the 12 to 13% the filings support with gains included.

Why three methods and not one. A ten-year median P/E assumes GATX is the same business it averaged over the window, and it is not: leverage is 3.3×, a third of the fleet arrived in January with about $3B of GABX debt, and gains are a bigger share of earnings. So P/E is 30%, not 100%. The two P/B-based legs ask what the equity underneath the earnings is worth: NAV marks the cars, justified P/B asks whether about 2.0× book is earned. The weights were set on method quality on 2026-10-01 with all legs visible; residual income is left at zero because it is justified P/B in another form, and the peer regression because R² is 0.03.

The inversion check, after the drop. At 16.34×, $162.78 implies 2027 EPS of $9.96, below my $10.18: the market now prices less than my earnings at the median multiple, which is why the expected return turned positive. It is not enough for a BUY; that needs a price below about $144. The P/B leg is most sensitive to the growth input: at 2% growth it is $127 and the target $155; at 4%, $143 and $161; at the model's sustainable 5%, $158 and $166. At the model's growth input the target is now above the price, but not 15% above it.

What the $162 excludes. The Brookfield call options. The first exercise on 2026-06-30 paid $66.2M for 3.5 points of GABX at a premium charged to equity. If used railcar values hold, the remaining tranches are worth something. Neither my target nor the Street's includes them, and they cut both ways: each buy-in adds debt-funded earnings.

EARNINGS BRIDGE AND SENSITIVITY

The Street takes 2027 EPS from $10.23 to $11.27, up $1.04. My model takes it from $10.29 to $10.18. Same starting business, different arithmetic on three lines. Repricing is the only line that helps, and it is small because only about a quarter of the fleet renews in a year and the acquired Wells pool is on longer, lower-rate leases that roll slowly. Interest is the line that hurts, and it is large because the 2026 maturities reprice from 3.05% to the high fives and the $1.9B GABX term loan still floats (the other $1B was refinanced into fixed notes at 4.63% and 5.30%).

SIGNPOSTS AND KILL CRITERIA

  • Q3 results, 2026-10-29. BMG $2.55 (model v39) vs FactSet consensus $2.46. I am above the Street on the quarter because H1 gains ran ahead of guide, and below it on 2027 because of interest. If Q3 prints above $2.55 with gains flat, my interest math is wrong and I say so.
  • Legacy disposition gains, FY2026. About $94M of legacy gain was booked in H1 (Lyons, Q2 call) against a $130M legacy guide; I carry $140M. Mgmt said full-year will exceed $130M "a little"; my $140M is in line. Below $146M the call moves toward SELL.
  • GATX's own LPI, quarter by quarter. +24.5%, +22.3%, +16.8%. Below +10% in any quarter is a kill criterion on the original thesis; for this call it confirms the fade. Above +20% again would say the repricing runway is longer than I model.
  • Renewal success with the LPI. 87% in 2025, 79.1% in Q1, 82.6% in Q2. One falling is mechanical. Both falling is price resistance.
  • Cost of new ten-year debt. The 2035 notes yield 5.95%, five basis points from the 6.00% trigger. Any ten-year issuance at or above 6.00% fires regardless of the tape.
  • Utilization below 96%, or a rating below BBB−. Both pre-registered. Neither is close: 98.0% and one notch of headroom (Fitch two).
  • The price itself. Above about $174 the rule moves the call to SELL; below about $144 to BUY. The target moved from $166 to $163 after the disposition basis was corrected (v39), not when the facts did; the direction never moved.

WHAT WOULD MAKE ME WRONG

  • The Street is right about interest. If GATX terms out the GABX loan cheaply, or rates fall, 2027 interest comes in below my $616M. Every $50M of interest is about $1.06 of EPS after tax, the same lever as gains. A 2027 print near $11.27 moves this toward BUY at the same multiple.
  • Book understates the fleet more than I mark it. GATX sells its oldest cars at 1.76× book. I mark the whole fleet at 1.04×. If the right mark is 1.14×, the target is $177; at 1.27× it is above the price. This is the single assumption a bull should attack, and it is on the page.
  • The multiple re-rates. GATX has closed at or above 17.8× on a quarter to a third of trading days in 2024, 2025 and 2026. At the five-year 75th percentile of 18.11× my own earnings are worth $190. I am not paying that for gains-heavy earnings, but the market has.
  • Dispositions stay at a plateau, not a peak. Used railcar prices are supported by a 25% tariff on imported tank cars, a backlog near a 15-year low, and scrapping that outruns builds. If $200M a year is the new normal rather than a cyclical high, my normalized-gains EPS of $7.81 is too low and the 22.0× argument weakens.
  • An accretive Brookfield buy-in. Each tranche moves earnings from the NCI line to GATX. The first cost $66M for 3.5 points. A larger exercise at a fair price is earnings I have not modeled.
  • The supply story is stronger than I give it credit for. 2025 orders were 20,361 against 35,000 to 40,000 of replacement need, the fleet is shrinking, and 135,000 cars hit the 50-year limit within five years. If LPI stays in the high teens into 2028 instead of fading, my bull case becomes the base.
  • Everyone else is a Buy. Four brokers, all Buy, average target $219.75. When I am the only bear, the burden of proof is mine. The proof is one number, 2027 interest, and it is checkable every quarter.
