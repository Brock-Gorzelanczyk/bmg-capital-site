The call in plain English

GATX buys railcars and leases them to shippers on multi-year contracts. In January it doubled its fleet by buying Wells Fargo's railcars with Brookfield, and took on about $4.5 billion of new debt to do it.

Lease rates are still rising, but only the cars whose leases end each year get a new rate. Meanwhile cheap debt is being refinanced at much higher rates. My model has 2027 earnings of $10.47 a share; the Street has $11.27. Most of the gap is interest.

I rated it SELL at $183.73. The stock fell 6.2% to $172.27 with my target unchanged, so my own rule now says HOLD: fairly priced to slightly rich, not cheap.

What drives the stock

Five drivers, most important first.

  1. 01
    Interest cost
    Debt went from $8.2B to $12.7B. Debt maturing in 2026 pays 3.05%; new debt costs 5.30%. 2027 interest of $616M is about $224M above 2025.
  2. 02
    Renewal pricing
    Renewals are still +16.8% over expiring rates, but slowing from +34% in 2023. They add about $45M of pretax profit in 2027, far less than the added interest.
  3. 03
    Fleet growth
    The Wells Fargo fleet doubled GATX's cars. GATX owns about a third of it (30% at close, 33.5% after buying more in June) and manages all of it. The Street assumes a second year of growth from the deal that I do not.
  4. 04
    Gains on selling old cars
    About a third of guided 2026 earnings. Real money, but it depends on used railcar prices, which move with the cycle.
  5. 05
    Railcar supply
    The North American fleet is 98% used and builders are cutting output. Tight supply keeps lease rates high, and a stronger economy is the main risk to my call.

How I built the model

Every input traces to a filing or a sourced estimate.

STEP 1
Start from the filings
Five years of history from the 10-K and Q2 10-Q, rebuilt into three linked statements.
STEP 2
Reprice the fleet
Revenue is cars times rate. Only cars whose leases expire get the new rate each year.
STEP 3
Rebuild the debt
Each bond and loan by maturity, refinanced at today's rates, so interest is built, not guessed.
STEP 4
Value it three ways
P/E at its 10-year median, net asset value of the fleet, and price to book against the return on equity.
STEP 5
Apply a fixed rule
BUY above +15% expected return, SELL below −5%, HOLD in between. Set before the model existed.
