WHY I WOULD BUY IT

  • Supply is running far below replacement and the fleet is shrinking. The industry ordered 20,361 railcars in 2025 against normalized replacement demand of 35,000 to 40,000, and the North American fleet contracted roughly 10,000 cars as scrapping outpaced new builds. Q2 2026 orders were approximately 5,800 units, down from 6,200 a year earlier. Manufacturers are cutting output to match rather than building into it. Lessors own 58% of the 1.65 million car fleet, up from 44% in 2008 — a structural shift that runs independent of the pricing cycle.
  • Tank cars are the tight end of that market, and GATX is weighted to them by value. Tank is 42% of Rail NA net book value against 33% of the car count, and chemicals and refined petroleum are roughly 49% of segment revenue. The industry tank car backlog is near a two-decade low. FreightCar America's CEO puts normal tank demand at 10,000 to 11,000 units a year and states installed US capacity cannot fulfil it; FreightCar's own tank work is DOT-111 retrofits, and it does not expect to enter new tank manufacturing until late 2027 at the earliest.
  • The commodity-car recovery is a tailwind here, not a threat. Covered hoppers are GATX's largest single freight exposure at 40.2% of the fleet, so the order recovery everyone is quoting lands in a segment where GATX is already the incumbent. GATX is the largest lessor in North America at roughly 22% of 950,000 lessor-owned cars.
  • The price increases are already locked in. Leases run about 56 months, so the fleet reprices in five groups, one each year. The group renewing now signed in 2021 and steps straight to today's rate, currently 16.8% higher, then locks for another five years. GATX holds $5.3B of contractual future lease receipts, of which $1,349M is already contracted for 2026 against $2.35B of expected revenue.
  • There is a maintenance synergy nobody is modeling. The acquired fleet spent ~$135M/year on maintenance, 100% of it at third-party shops. GATX performs 76% of its own repairs in-house. Moving even part of $135M of third-party spend in-house is margin that appears in no model.
  • They bought a huge fleet with a small check. Wells Fargo sold its 101,000-car railcar leasing business at $4.2B. GATX partnered with Brookfield Infrastructure in a JV called GABX: Brookfield took 70%, GATX put in ~$400M for 30%. GATX manages the entire fleet and holds annual options to buy Brookfield out over ~10 years. They get paid to run assets they mostly do not own.

Where I sit versus the Street: below it. My earnings are consensus — FactSet has 2027 at $11.27, I am at $11.25 — but my target is below all three published targets of $218 to $221. Those imply 19.4× to 19.6×, inside the top decile of GATX's own ten-year range. The disagreement is not about earnings. It is that the Street is underwriting a re-rating the stock has rarely delivered.

What the $200 excludes. GATX holds annual options to acquire Brookfield's 70% of GABX over ~10 years, having contributed ~$400M for 30% of a fleet purchased at $4.2B. The market prices those options at zero. If used tank car values hold — the premise of this note — they are worth something. Neither published targets nor mine include them.

EARNINGS BRIDGE AND SENSITIVITY

Consensus has pretax income going from $401M in 2026 to $446M in 2027, up $45M. I rebuilt it from the company's own disclosures. At a 56-month lease term, 21% of a given lease's term reprices annually (12 ÷ 56); against the legacy fleet count that's approximately 23% renewing per year, and against the combined Rail NA fleet ~12%. The 56-month term produces roughly 4.7 vintage groups, not five — each renewal steps to today's rate, currently 16.8% higher, then locks for another cycle.

SIGNPOSTS AND CATALYSTS

  • Tank car orders, quarterly. Roughly 1,157 in Q2 2026, derived each quarter from FreightCar America's disclosed total share and ex-tank-car share. Above ~2,500 would mean tank car supply is finally responding, and that is what would make me wrong.
  • GATX's own LPI trajectory, quarter by quarter. Q4 2025 +24.5%, Q1 2026 +22.3%, Q2 2026 +16.8% — already decelerating and now sitting at the floor of management's own 2026 guide of "high teens to low 20%." Below +12% in any single quarter is faster than the guide implied and would say the pricing mechanism is weakening. This is a GATX-only monitor; the prior version of this signpost compared GATX to a peer using metrics with non-overlapping windows and opposite direction, which is a falsified comparison per the vault ledger. Do not reintroduce.
  • Renewal success and the Lease Price Index together. Success was 87% in 2025 [L03] and is now in the low 80s (Q1 2026 79.1%, Q2 2026 82.6%) while the LPI fell from +24.5% to +16.8%. One falling is mechanical. Both falling is real price resistance.
  • Chemical and petroleum carloads, weekly. Running +2.2% and +5.7% year to date. The AAR publishes them free every week — if both turn negative the squeeze ends from the demand side, not the supply side.
  • The first Brookfield call option exercise. Until GATX exercises one, the market prices that option at zero. An accretive exercise is the event that forces a rethink of the earnings mix.
  • Q3 results. Consensus about $2.46. I would be modestly above.

WHAT COULD GO WRONG

  • Industry book-to-bill moved above 1.0× in Q2 2026, the first time this cycle, so the backlog has stopped shrinking. It happened through production cuts rather than an order surge, but supply can respond faster than it looks.
  • Renewal pricing is running at the bottom of management's own guidance. Q2 came in at 16.8% — at or just under the floor of the "high teens to low 20%" range. Missing your own guidance on the metric the thesis depends on is the signal to watch, and renewal success falling from 87% in 2025 [L03] toward the low 80s (Q1 2026 79.1%, Q2 2026 82.6%) says the same thing.
  • Disposition gains are guided up, not down, and that is the fragile line. Management guided $200M against $130M in 2025. The ten-year legacy average was $79M. Fleet-adjusted normal is roughly $153M. It is the least contractual earnings line GATX has and is at a five-year high.
  • It is very levered, and the refinancing arithmetic matters. $12.5B of debt against $2.8B of equity, of which approximately $3.8B is floating (31%) including the $2,959M GABX term loan at ~5.02% repricing continuously. The 2026 maturity tranche carries a 3.04% weighted-average coupon; the marginal rate on new ten-year notes priced March 2026 was 5.30% — a 226 bp step-up, not 50. GATX earns the gap between what it pays to borrow and what a railcar earns. The blended book rate near 4.8% is at parity with peer blended rates (the earlier draft's peer-advantage claim compared GATX's blended rate to a peer's recourse-only rate, a basis mismatch falsified per the vault ledger; do not reintroduce). Real risk: if funding costs rise faster than lease rates, the spread narrows on every refinanced tranche as well as every new car.
  • Consensus expects deliveries to recover above 34,000 units in 2027. An 18-month horizon still runs into the window the recovery is forecast to arrive.
  • Europe is not recovering. Rail International is about $2.6B of ~$18.5B in net book value. Management said they entered 2025 hoping conditions would improve and "it did not."
  • Estimates are drifting down and everyone is already bullish. All five analyst ratings are Buy; three carry published numeric targets of $218 to $221 — the other two are unpublished. There is no bear left to convert.
